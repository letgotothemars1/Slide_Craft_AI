# SlideCraft AI
<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->
## Platform
web
## Users
Master’s students preparing academic presentations from an assignment, an existing AI discussion, and one PDF.
## Product purpose
Review visible, editable content while generation proceeds; approve the draft and choose a theme before AI plans and checks final slides for editable PowerPoint.
## Confirmed constraints
Five English slides, one shared PDF, three themes, native text and shapes. Source suggestions require human checking. Local secrets must never be read or committed. Existing one-shot generation at `/generate` stays available.
## Approved workflow
Materials → whole-deck outline for five slides → actual streaming content with deck context in a neutral draft → review and save independent sections and speaker notes (organize older slide text if needed) → compare three actual-content compositions → Approve & style → choose one of three themes → Generate final slides with AI → review designed slides → Download PowerPoint. Keep slide thumbnails, the central artifact canvas, and a separate inspector.

Before styling, each accepted slide receives exactly three persisted variants from one AI request using its actual content and notes: a recommended option, an alternative and an out-of-the-box option. One is selected and shown in the main canvas; the three cards include this selected option rather than adding a fourth. Selection survives reload. Content fingerprints invalidate alternatives after accepted text/notes change, and guarded workers cannot replace newer edits. Older slides have an explicit generation action; prepared key-free alternatives are honestly labelled.

Speaker notes are generated with initial content and exported as native PowerPoint notes with source metadata. They now have a readable Notes tab beside Content and Sources in the external inspector, with a separate edit action. On desktop the slide stays visible above independently scrolling slide controls, while the rail and notes inspector scroll separately; on narrow screens notes follow the slide before variants. Save or discard pending edits before styling. Older slides without notes show an explicit empty state.
Approve & style opens three theme examples using the actual selected slide. Choosing an example does not recolor the neutral content draft; the selected palette appears as final design candidates are saved. After approval, AI analyses the complete narrative with assignment, context, all accepted slides and notes, then plans validated native composition actions per stable slide id. The executable catalog has balanced, feature, bands and poster arrangements within six reviewed layout families: hero, editorial, chart, process, comparison and statement. The server validates stable slide and section ids and executes structured actions in a bounded workflow. Covers stay hero, comparisons stay comparison, and accepted bars/processes retain chart/process and their exact labels, values and units. Ordinary text uses editorial or statement. The selected theme background stays consistent; AI cannot invert it. Accepted title, body, section headings/text, visuals, notes and source text survive styling. Completed designs survive failure; retry only affected slides. Saved visible-text or section edits invalidate that slide’s design before export.

New AI drafts include one to four ordered sections with stable ids, headings, and their own text/data in the same content call. Older slides offer Organize slide sections and a selected-slide grouping action. Grouping partitions the accepted body without adding, dropping, or reordering words; review its headings and grouping before design. Each section can be edited independently, and clicking its slide text opens the corresponding inspector field. Section edits save together with revision checks and keep the legacy body in sync. Whole-body AI revision clears previous section bindings for renewed review.

A separate whole-deck semantic-agreement check compares visible content with notes before AI design, including numbers, units, qualifiers, unsupported material assertions and essential visible content. Blocking findings stop design for user correction without rewriting accepted text. The fingerprint cache applies only to unchanged content; template mode explicitly lacks AI semantic checking. Final AI design starts from the selected persisted variant; refinements update that same member so its preview remains identical to the main slide, without an extra fourth candidate. Every AI slide receives at least one refinement and two visual reviews, even when its first candidate passes. Mechanical fit and configured vision critique feed at most three per-slide reviews and two refinements. A final rendered-deck review checks coherence and can request one bounded targeted repair per slide followed by one deck recheck. Candidate comparison, per-slide critique and deck review assess quantitative discoverability, purposeful hierarchy and allocation of space to actual content length. Spoken explanations are not automatically missing visible content. Detailed findings stay internal; the editor shows whole saved candidates, progress and concise retry controls. An unresolved result cannot become ready or export. The active thumbnail shows a shimmer and a local Writing, Organizing, Revising or Designing label; reduced motion uses a static tint.

The browser preview and editable PowerPoint use the same text-and-rectangle scene geometry. Chart values must occur in both the accepted body and supplied PDF excerpts. This checks provenance, not factual accuracy, unit/category meaning, or whether a suggested excerpt supports the claim; users still check sources.

## Progress and generation limits
The staged workflow durably saves its stage and an observable action log. New projects default to 60 logical model requests; explicitly stored older limits remain unchanged. The historical T31 five-slide base workflow used 29 requests and its bounded worst case used 50. T32 adds variant generation and semantic agreement while removing redundant model selection for a selected variant. The persisted cap bounds all current operations, including three reviews per slide, one deck-driven repair per slide and one deck recheck. Additional user regeneration shares the remaining budget. Outline/story/design planning, content, variant generation, semantic agreement, vision reviews, refinements, deck review and manual AI revision/grouping consume this budget. Local inspection, composition execution, rendering, mechanical checks, and export do not consume model requests. Returned provider token usage is saved when available; missing usage remains unknown. These counters describe application requests and returned usage, not billing. Transport retries, PDF embeddings, legacy outline/build endpoints and the existing one-shot generator are outside the staged counters.

Key-free template draft and final design make no model calls. They use the native composition catalog, real PNG rendering, and mechanical review; final template design does not claim vision model review. Explicit manual AI actions remain available, can use a configured model, and are counted.

## Intake continuity
Assignment, context pack, and an already-uploaded PDF reference are saved automatically in browser localStorage. Initial intake has no theme choice; legacy stored theme values are ignored for new intake creation. Theme choice is deferred until accepted content is ready for final design. Choose AI or key-free generation on the intake form; creation and draft start happen before opening the editor. Start from scratch clears saved assignment/context, removes the PDF reference, and clears the cached project used for retries. Reload remains blank when browser storage is available; previously created projects are retained.
